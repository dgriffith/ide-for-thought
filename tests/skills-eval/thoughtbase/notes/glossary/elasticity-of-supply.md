# Elasticity of supply

The **price elasticity of housing supply** measures how much the quantity of
housing built responds to a change in price. Formally, the percentage change in
quantity supplied divided by the percentage change in price:

$$\varepsilon_s = \frac{\%\,\Delta Q}{\%\,\Delta P}.$$

A metro with **elastic** supply (high $\varepsilon_s$) answers a demand shock by
building — prices barely move. An **inelastic** metro (low $\varepsilon_s$),
constrained by geography or zoning, answers the same shock with price spikes
because quantity cannot adjust.

Housing supply elasticity varies enormously across cities and is the hinge of
the argument in [[notes/upzoning-lowers-rents]]: upzoning is, in effect, a way
to raise $\varepsilon_s$.
