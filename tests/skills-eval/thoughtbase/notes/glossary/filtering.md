# Filtering

**Filtering** is the process by which housing units move *down* the price
ladder as they age. A unit built today as high-end housing becomes mid-market in
twenty years and affordable in forty, as newer, more desirable stock is built
above it and the original occupants move up and out.

It is the mechanism that lets new market-rate construction — even luxury
construction — eventually ease rents for lower-income renters, without any unit
ever being *built* as "affordable." Filtering is slow (decades), can stall when
a neighborhood gentrifies (units filter *up*), and depends on a steady stream of
new supply at the top to push the ladder down.

Contrast with [[notes/glossary/elasticity-of-supply]], which describes how
*responsive* new supply is to price in the first place.
