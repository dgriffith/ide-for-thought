# Why rent control fixes affordability

The whole case rests on one step: **because a cap holds each tenant's rent
below the market rate, capping rents makes a city affordable.** Everything else
follows from that — if sitting tenants pay less, the city is cheaper, so cap
rents and you are done.

Tenants under a cap do pay less than they otherwise would. Their disposable
income rises; displacement of long-tenured residents slows. These are real,
measurable effects and the argument leans its full weight on them.

The move from "existing tenants pay less" to "the city is affordable" is doing
all the work and is never examined. It quietly assumes the set of people who
need housing is the set of people who already have a capped unit — ignoring
newcomers, the queue, and what the cap does to how many units exist at all. Pull
that one plank and the structure has nothing left to stand on.
