Diagrams

You can draw diagrams right inside a note — flowcharts, sequence diagrams, and more — by describing them in a simple text syntax. When you preview the note, your description turns into a clean, finished diagram, so you never have to fiddle with boxes and arrows by hand.

Writing a diagram

Add a cell labelled mermaid and describe the diagram inside it. Each line names a step and how it connects to the next. To drop one in quickly, right-click in the editor and choose Elements → Mermaid Diagram; you'll get an empty cell with your cursor ready inside.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  A[Draft] --> B{Reviewed?}
  B -- yes --> C[Published]
  B -- no --> A
```

Kinds of diagrams

Many diagram styles are supported — flowcharts, sequence diagrams, class and state diagrams, entity relationships, timelines, and more. Two of the most useful:

Flowchart
Boxes and arrows. Show steps and how they branch. You can lay it out left-to-right (LR) or top-down (TD). 
```mermaid
flowchart TD
  Start --> Decision{OK?}
  Decision -->|yes| End
  Decision -->|no| Start
```
Sequence diagram
Who talks to whom. Show the participants and the messages passing between them, in order. 
```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
  Alice->>Bob: Question
  Bob-->>Alice: Answer
```
Diagrams match your theme

Diagrams use the same colors as the note around them. Switch between light, dark, or high-contrast, and your diagrams redraw to match — there's nothing extra to set up.

Mindmaps of notes

A :::mindmap block draws a hierarchy of notes as a mindmap, out from one note. Name that note as a link on a line of its own, and say how its branches are joined:

:::mindmap
[[Launch plan]]
relation: parent
depth: 3
:::
relation
How a note's branches are joined to it. A field that links to another note, such as parent or part-of: a note's branches are the notes whose field points at it. decomposes: the notes that decompose it. argument: the claims that support, rebut or otherwise bear on it, each labelled with how. headings, or leaving it out: the note's own headings and list items.
direction
out follows the field the other way, for one that points down at a note's parts rather than up at its parent.
depth
How many levels to draw, 1 to 6 (3 if you leave it out). A branch with more beneath it says how many.
layout
radial draws it around the note in the middle; otherwise it reads left to right.

A note with more than one parent is drawn under each, saying how many places it appears; a hierarchy that loops back on itself stops where it comes round, and says so. Double-click a branch, or press Enter on it, to open its note. Fold, zoom and move around it as you would a note's own mindmap; the block itself can't be edited, since it's drawn from many notes.
