Back / forward

As you move between notes, sources, and queries, Minerva remembers where you've been — so you can retrace your steps. Press ⌘[ to go back and ⌘] to go forward (use Ctrl on Windows and Linux), or click the two arrows in the title bar. It works just like the back and forward buttons in a web browser.

The controls

⌘[
Back. Returns you to the previous place you were. Dimmed when there's nothing behind you.
⌘]
Forward. Returns you to a place you just came back from. Dimmed once you've caught back up to where you started.
Title-bar arrows
Two arrow buttons at the left edge of the title bar do the same thing as the shortcuts. Each dims when there's nowhere to go in that direction.

Going back doesn't just reopen a note — it returns you to the exact spot you were reading, not the top of the note. Sources and queries are remembered the same way, so you can step back out of a query or a source you were looking at just as easily.

What counts as a step

Most ways of moving around leave a trail you can walk back along, but a couple don't:

Clicking in the sidebar
Remembered. Opening a note from the sidebar records where you came from and where you landed.
Following a wiki-link
Remembered. Clicking a [[link]] to hop from one note to another builds a trail, so you can walk right back out of a chain of linked notes.
Quick switch (⌘P)
Remembered. Jumping to a note, source, or saved query from Quick Switch records a step, just like clicking it in the sidebar.
A search result
Remembered. Jumping to a search match records a step, so Back returns you to where you were before the search.
Back and forward themselves
Retracing your steps doesn't add new ones, so you can step back and forth freely without cluttering the trail.
Going somewhere new clears the way forward

If you step back a few notes and then head off in a new direction — clicking a link or opening something from the sidebar — the forward trail is cleared, just like in a web browser. To revisit those notes you'd navigate to them again.

Your trail is per-project: opening a different thoughtbase starts fresh with an empty history.
