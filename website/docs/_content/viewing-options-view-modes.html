View modes

You can look at a note as the plain text you type, as the finished, formatted page, as a mindmap of its outline, or with the text beside either one. Each pane sets its own mode, so you can be editing one note while reading another in the same window. Switching modes never changes your note — it's just a different way to look at the same content.

The modes

The mode belongs to the pane, not the note. Reopen a note later and it appears in whatever mode that pane was last set to.

Source
The plain text you type, ready to edit. This is where you'll want to be for hands-on work: fixing a link, adjusting a note's properties, or writing inside a cell.
Preview
The finished, read-only page, with everything formatted the way it will look — headings, callouts, math, diagrams, charts, and embedded media all shown live.
Side by side
Your text on the left and the formatted page on the right, in the same pane, so you can write and see the result at once. Chosen from the Mindmap, it puts your text beside the map instead.
Mindmap
The note's outline as a map: its title in the middle, its headings and list items as branches. You can reshape the note from here. See Editing a note as a mindmap below.

A plain-text file has nothing to format, so it only shows Source — the toggle doesn't appear.

Switching modes

There are two ways to change a pane's mode:

Toolbar buttons
Three buttons above the note — Source, Side by side, Preview, Mindmap — jump straight to that mode from wherever you are, so you can go from editing to the finished page in one click.
Cycle shortcut — ⌘⇧P
Steps the active pane through the modes in order: Source → Preview → Side by side → Mindmap → Source. The same command is in the View menu as Cycle Preview Mode.
Mindmap — ⌘⇧M
Shows the note as a mindmap, and again goes back to the editor. In the View menu as Mindmap.

Editing a note as a mindmap

The map is the note's outline: the note's title is the centre, its headings branch from it by level, and the list items under each heading branch from that heading by indentation. Frontmatter, code, tables and the rest of your writing aren't on the map, and nothing you do on the map touches them.

Every change you make on the map is a change to the note's text, made the same way as if you'd typed it: ⌘Z undoes it, on the map or in the editor, and the note shows as unsaved until you save it.

Tab
Add a child to the selected branch and name it. Under a heading with list items, it's a list item; otherwise a heading one level down.
Enter
Add a branch after the selected one, at its level, and name it. A new branch you leave unnamed goes again.
F2 or double-click
Rename the branch.
Delete
Remove the branch. With branches under it, Minerva asks first, and they go too.
⌘[ / ⌘]
Promote the branch out a level, to just after the branch it was in, or demote it under the branch before it. Everything under it moves with it.
Drag
Drop a branch on the middle of another to move it in, or on its top or bottom edge to move it before or after.
Arrows, Space
↑ and ↓ move between branches at the same level, ← to the parent and → to the first child; Space folds or unfolds a branch, which then shows how many it hides. ⇧Tab leaves the map.

Headings and list items stay what they are: a list item can't be promoted into a heading, a heading can't move into a list, and nothing goes deeper than a sixth-level heading. When an edit can't be made, Minerva says why.

Choose Left to right or Radial for the map's shape. Scroll to zoom, drag the background to move around, and Fit to see it all. Which branches are folded, and the map's shape, are kept for each note on this computer, not in the note.

With Side by side, the text and the map follow each other: select a branch and the editor's cursor moves to its line; move the cursor and the map selects the branch it's in.
