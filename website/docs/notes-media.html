Media

Media in Minerva means YouTube. Put a video URL in a youtube fence and the preview draws a poster card — the video's thumbnail with a play button — that opens the video in your default browser when you click it. Minerva deliberately doesn't run a live player inside the app: no third-party frame or script ever loads in your notes, and nothing is fetched from YouTube until you actually view a note containing an embed.

The youtube fence

A media embed is a fenced code block whose language is youtube. The first non-empty line is the video URL; any lines after it become an optional caption, shown on the card in place of the default "Watch on YouTube" label.

```youtube
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aBcDeFgHiJk
Feynman on how rubber bands work
```

To insert one, right-click in the editor and choose Elements → YouTube Video — it drops an empty fence with the cursor inside, ready for a pasted URL.

Accepted URL forms

Paste the URL in whatever shape YouTube gave you — the address bar, the Share button, or an embed snippet all work. Minerva recognizes:

youtube.com/watch?v=ID
Standard watch URL. The address-bar form. Extra query parameters (&list=, &si=, &feature=, …) are tolerated and stripped.
youtu.be/ID
Share link. The short form the Share button copies.
…/shorts/ID
Shorts. A Short embeds like any other video.
…/embed/ID …/live/ID …/v/ID
Embed, live, and legacy paths. Useful when you're pasting a URL dug out of an <iframe> snippet. The privacy domain youtube-nocookie.com is accepted too.

www. and m. prefixes and http vs. https don't matter. Whatever form you paste, Minerva normalizes it to a clean youtube.com/watch?v=ID link with tracking parameters removed — that normalized URL is what opens when you click the card.

Timestamps

A t= or start= parameter survives normalization, so a "start at 1:30" link keeps its offset. Both the bare-seconds form (t=90) and the colloquial forms (t=90s, t=2m30s, t=1h2m3s) are understood; the canonical link becomes watch?v=ID&t=90s.

How the card renders

In preview, the fence becomes a poster card: the video's thumbnail with a play button over it, your caption (or "Watch on YouTube") beneath, and a youtube.com host line so it's obvious where a click leads. Clicking the card — or focusing it and pressing Enter; it's an ordinary link — opens the video in your default browser, at the timestamp if the URL carried one.

Why not an inline player?

An embedded player means YouTube's scripts and cookies running inside your knowledge base. Minerva's renderer forbids third-party frames outright, so the card is the whole story: the only network request is for the static thumbnail image, made when you view the note, and playback happens in your browser where you already manage that relationship.

On export

The interactive card is meaningless in an exported file, so when you publish or export a note each youtube fence degrades to a linked thumbnail — the poster image wrapped in a link to the video, [![caption](thumbnail)](watch-url) in markdown terms. A reader of the exported artifact still sees the poster and can click through to watch.

Gotchas

  • Paste a full URL, scheme and all. A bare video id or a schemeless youtube.com/watch?v=… doesn't parse — the fence renders an "Unrecognized YouTube URL" error in its place. Copy the complete https:// URL from the browser or the Share button. The error is inline and harmless; fix the URL and the card appears.
  • One video per fence. Only the first non-empty line is read as the URL. A second URL on a later line isn't a second embed — it's caption text.
  • Videos only, not playlists or channels. Playlist, channel, and @handle URLs don't resolve. A watch URL that carries a &list= parameter embeds fine, but the playlist context is dropped — the card links to that single video.
  • No title lookup. Fetching the real title would mean calling YouTube every time the note renders, which Minerva won't do. The card shows your caption or the generic "Watch on YouTube" — write a caption if the note should read well.
  • Offline, the thumbnail is blank. The poster image comes from img.youtube.com when the note is viewed. Without a network the card still renders — caption, play button, working link — just without the picture, and the image fills in next time you view the note online.
