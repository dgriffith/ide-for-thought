Proposals

The Proposals panel is where the LLM proposes, the human confirms — the most important design decision in Minerva — becomes a screen you actually look at. Every graph write an AI operation wants to make waits here as a reviewable card until you approve or reject it; nothing lands in .minerva/graph.ttl on the strength of a model's say-so alone.

What shows up here

A proposal is a thought:Proposal node created by the approval engine (src/main/llm/approval.ts) whenever an LLM operation's approval tier is requires_approval — new claims, evidence links, component creation, note refactors/deletes, in-place note rewrites, and proposed source properties all file this way by default. Each card bundles one or more payloads — a new note, a block of graph triples, or both — that apply atomically: approve lands the whole bundle, reject discards the whole bundle, never half of one.

Not scoped to the open note

Unlike every other panel in the right sidebar, Proposals is not filtered to the file you currently have open — ProposalsPanel.svelte is handed only a revision counter to know when to refresh, not an activeFilePath. It lists every proposal in the project regardless of which note is active, with a status-tab filter (All / Pending / Approved / Rejected) defaulting to All so the panel doubles as an audit trail — most everyday AI operations (auto-tag, auto-link) are actually autonomous or notify_only tier and apply immediately with nothing to review, so a panel that only ever showed "Pending" would look empty most of the time.

Reviewing a proposal

Click a card to expand it. Each payload is a separate collapsible row — click one to preview its actual content: a note's full text, or the raw Turtle for a graph-triples payload. This is the diff view: you're reading exactly what will be written, not a paraphrase of it, before you decide.

Approve — y
Applies the proposal's full payload bundle via the approval engine's approveProposal() and flips its status to approved. The keystroke only fires while a card is expanded (selected) — handleKeydown checks selectedUri first. A success banner reports exactly what landed (note paths included, up to three, then "+N more").
Reject — n
Calls rejectProposal(), which flips status to rejected and applies nothing. The proposal node stays in the graph as a rejected record — rejecting doesn't delete it, so the audit trail shows what the AI suggested even when you turned it down.
Close — s / Esc
Collapses the expanded card without changing anything. Pure UI state, not a decision.
Status tabs
All / Pending / Approved / Rejected, filtering the list client-side by the proposal's stored status. Search and a Newest-first / By-type sort sit above the tabs for finding one proposal in a long history.

Why some AI writes never appear here

Not every LLM operation is a proposal. The approval engine classifies each operation type into one of three tiers, and only requires_approval ever creates a card you review:

requires_approval
New claims, evidence links, component creation, note refactors and deletes, note rewrites, source-property proposals. Filed as a pending thought:Proposal and held until you act on it.
notify_only
Confidence updates, status changes. Applied immediately, but still recorded as an already-approved proposal — so it shows up here under the Approved tab as a record of what happened, not a decision to make.
autonomous
Tag additions, staleness flags. Applied immediately with no proposal node at all — nothing to find in this panel, at any status.

One escalation cuts across all three: any write that touches a node already marked thought:hasStatus thought:established is forced up to requires_approval regardless of its own tier, so the AI can't quietly re-tag or re-flag something you've already vetted.

Gotchas

  • "Expiry" isn't a background timer. CLAUDE.md describes proposals as auto-expiring after "a configurable window," and every proposal does carry an autoExpires timestamp — seven days out by default (write.expiryDays ?? 7 in approval.ts). But nothing sweeps the graph on a schedule to enforce it: expireProposals() only flips overdue pending proposals to expired when something actually calls it (wired to the proposal:expire IPC channel). A stale pending proposal past its window will keep showing as Pending in the panel — not silently vanish — until that check runs.
  • Approve and Reject are no-ops on anything but a pending proposal. Both buttons are disabled once a card is already approved, rejected, or expired (tooltip reads "Already {status}"), and the underlying IPC calls return false for the same reason rather than erroring — the panel surfaces that as "may already be approved/rejected, or its payload has gone stale."
  • Approving with an empty payload bundle throws, not silently succeeds. approveProposal() deliberately refuses to flip status on a proposal with zero payloads — that shape means something upstream filed the proposal wrong, and the engine would rather surface the bug than approve a no-op.
  • Editing or deleting the target note first doesn't invalidate the proposal. A note payload writes to a relativePath at approval time, resolved fresh (suffixed -2, -3, … on a collision); a graph-triples payload merges Turtle into the store regardless of what's since changed on disk. If the note you were editing when a proposal was filed has since moved or been deleted, approving still applies the bundle against the project as it is now, which can land somewhere you don't expect — review the expanded payload preview rather than assuming it still matches what prompted the proposal.
