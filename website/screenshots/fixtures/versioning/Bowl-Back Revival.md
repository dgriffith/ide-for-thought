# The Bowl-Back Revival

The Neapolitan bowl-back all but vanished from working bands after 1920, and for
sixty years it was a museum instrument. It is coming back — slowly, and mostly
from the early-music side rather than from bluegrass.

## Why it fell out of favour

The carved-top Gibson F-5 was louder in a string band, easier to hold standing
up, and easier to build in quantity. Against a banjo, volume settled the
argument.

## What the revival players want

Not volume. A bowl-back gives a rounder, shorter note that suits Vivaldi and
Neapolitan song far better than a carved top does — the repertoire came first
and the instrument followed it back.
