# The Gibson Revolution

At the turn of the twentieth century, **Orville Gibson** reimagined the
mandolin. Instead of bending thin ribs into a fragile bowl, he *carved* the
top and back from solid wood, the way a violin is made.

## Why it mattered

- A carved, arched top is louder and more durable
- The archtop **F5** became the voice of American bluegrass
- Lloyd Loar's 1920s signed instruments set the standard still chased today

The flatter, sturdier body suited the stage — and Bill Monroe's chop.
