# The Mandolin, in Brief

A **mandolin** is a small, double-strung member of the lute family, tuned in
fifths like a violin — G, D, A, E — with eight strings in four courses. Its
bright, sustaining voice comes from a *tremolo*: rapid alternate picking that
turns a plucked note into a singing line.

Three families dominate the instrument's history:

- The **bowl-back Neapolitan**, refined by the Vinaccia workshop.
- The **carved-top Gibson** A- and F-series, born in early-1900s Michigan.
- The **flat-back** folk instruments of Ireland and the Americas.
